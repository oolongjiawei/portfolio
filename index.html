Data analysis · Dashboards · Team of 6 2025

Toronto Building Permits, 1991–2025

For George Brown's data-storytelling course, our team analyzed 35 years of the City of Toronto's open building-permit data and built an operational dashboard and an executive scorecard in Tableau for a permit-services client.

  • 19,995 permits issued in 2024 (−3,612 YoY), while average time to issuance improved to ~51 days (−15.5%).
  • A classic 80/20: about 20% of permit types account for ~80% of total permit duration.
  • New Building permits are the slowest, often more than a year from application to issuance.
  • Activity clusters in a few neighbourhoods: Willowdale, East York, Greektown, the Annex and Dufferin Grove.
  • Tableau
  • TabPy (Python)
  • KPIs & YoY
  • What-if parameters
  • Pareto
  • Clustering
  • Regression

02 — About

From rigs to interfaces

“Technical complexity means nothing if the user experience fails.”

A rig is a user interface for animators. If the controls are confusing, the most elegant joint hierarchy in the world still slows the whole show down. Five years of that taught me to treat every structure as an experience someone has to live with.

I retrained through BrainStation's software engineering bootcamp, where our team won the Microsoft hackathon for the most user-friendly design, and George Brown's Applied AI program (Honours), where I worked with Python, SQL and Tableau to turn open data into dashboards and stories. At Kinectrics I built and redesigned interface screens for an internal AI tool.

I'm looking for front-end developer or data analyst roles — teams where how something looks, and what the numbers say, both matter.
